How Texas home insurance pays for an older roof

This page is general information, not legal or insurance advice. Your own policy decides what is covered. All facts below come from the Texas Department of Insurance (TDI); each one links to the TDI page it comes from.

Hail and wind are the main reasons roofs get replaced in South Texas, and most of those roofs are paid for, at least in part, by a homeowners policy. Two lines in that policy decide how much you actually get back: whether your roof is covered at replacement cost or actual cash value, and how your deductible is figured. Both matter more as a roof gets older.

Replacement cost vs. actual cash value

TDI explains that most home policies pay to repair or rebuild "based on current costs." That is replacement cost coverage. Some policies instead pay actual cash value, which subtracts depreciation for the age and condition of what was damaged. Actual cash value policies cost less, but they also pay less when you have a claim (TDI: replacement cost or actual cash value?).

TDI's own example uses a house insured for $200,000 with a 2% ($4,000) deductible and a storm that ruins the whole roof. A new roof costs $10,000.

Roof ageReplacement cost policy paysActual cash value policy pays
5 years$6,000$4,500
10 years$6,000$3,000
20 years$6,000$0

In TDI's example the actual cash value of the 20-year-old roof is $4,000, exactly the deductible, so the policy pays nothing. Same storm, same roof, same house.

Why your roof's age matters at renewal

TDI's roof-replacement guidance says that "as roofs age, some companies will switch to actual cash value," and that your company should tell you when your coverage changes at renewal. If a roof is in poor condition, the company might not cover the roof at all. TDI also reminds homeowners that insurance will not pay for a new roof just because it is old or worn out; coverage is for damage from a covered event such as a storm (TDI: replacing your roof).

The same page notes that a company may inspect your roof when you apply, and may charge you more or refuse to insure you based on what it finds. Reading your renewal notice every year is the simplest way to catch a switch from replacement cost to actual cash value before a storm, not after.

Percentage deductibles: do the math in dollars

A deductible "may be a specific dollar amount or a percentage," and TDI suggests turning any percentage into dollars before you choose a policy (TDI: deductibles). In TDI's example, a home is insured for $150,000. One policy has a $500 deductible; another has a 5% deductible, which is $7,500. If roof repairs cost $6,500, the first policy pays $6,000 and the second pays nothing.

TDI also suggests asking your agent whether your policy has a separate wind and hail deductible that is different from the deductible for other damage, because that can mean paying more yourself after a storm (TDI: replacing your roof).

How a replacement cost claim is usually paid

TDI explains that replacement cost claims are often paid in two checks. The first check is partial; the rest is paid after repairs are under way. Keep your contract, invoices and photos together so you can show the work was done (TDI: replacing your roof).

Deadlines on both sides

TDI's home insurance guide says most companies have deadlines for filing a claim, and some policies have a one-year deadline unless you can show good cause for the delay. Check your own policy for the exact rule (TDI: home insurance guide).

The same guide lists the insurer's side of the clock. In general, the company must acknowledge your claim within 15 days, accept or deny it within 15 business days after it gets the information it needs (longer after major disasters), and pay within 5 business days after agreeing to pay. TDI also advises filing "as soon as you can" after hail or wind damage (TDI: after hail or windstorms).

Questions to ask your agent before the next storm

  • Is my roof covered at replacement cost or actual cash value?
  • Will that change at my next renewal because of the roof's age?
  • What is my wind and hail deductible, in dollars?
  • Is it different from my deductible for other damage?
  • How long do I have to report storm damage under my policy?

For questions about a claim or your policy, TDI's consumer help line is 800-252-3439. For the Texas rules on roofers and deductibles, see our roof insurance claim basics, and for what hail damage looks like, see hail and storm damage.
